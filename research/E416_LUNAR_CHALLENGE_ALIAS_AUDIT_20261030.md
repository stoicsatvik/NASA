# E416 | Official-site lunar challenge identity ambiguity

**30 October 2026.** Independent pre-event source audit, correcting the interpretation of E411. No competition solution, submission, registration, outreach or dataset modification.

## Question

E411 showed a **literal title mismatch** between the 14-title HackAthlone index (`Lunar Landing Site Navigator`) and E193's historical analyst entry (`CLPS lunar browser`). E411 interpreted this as a stale, different challenge and required a new rating. **Does the title mismatch itself establish that these are substantively different challenges?**

## Fresh source evidence (30 October 2026)

1. The official NASA Space Apps [Lunar Landing Site Navigator challenge page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/lunar-landing-site-navigator/) describes Moon south-pole landing-site/date comparison, Sun and Earth horizon geometry, solar power potential and direct-to-Earth communication windows. The indexed page was available in web search; direct full-page retrieval returned HTTP 502.
2. The same official NASA Space Apps platform's [lunabase404 team page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/find-a-team/lunabase404/) labels its challenge **CLPS Lunar Mission Browser**, but the platform's `About the Challenge` section describes the **same four task features** (south-polar lunar mission planning, Sun/Earth horizon geometry, site/date comparison, power and direct-to-Earth communication). Search-indexed text was visible; direct page retrieval returned HTTP 502.
3. A second independent team record, [Apollo 416](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/find-a-team/apollo-416/), and additional official-platform team records such as [Datanauts](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/find-a-team/datanauts2/) also label the task **CLPS Lunar Mission Browser** and display the same `About the Challenge` description.
4. A third-party [Rzeszów 2026 event listing](https://whatdahack.pl/events/nasa-space-apps-challenge-rzeszow-2026) lists **both** names as entries with near-identical task descriptions. This could be duplicate/alias ingestion or two separately enumerated challenge identifiers; it is **not** official canonical identity proof.

These are **independently published page observations**, not measured participant outcomes. The official team pages contain user-authored team descriptions, but their separately rendered `Challenge` and `About the Challenge` fields are platform metadata. No claim of access to an official challenge-ID mapping is made.

## Falsification

- **SUPPORTED:** E411's exact string mismatch, 13/14 literal alias mapping under its hardcoded dictionary.
- **NOT SUPPORTED:** the stronger inference that the historical `CLPS lunar browser` necessarily refers to a *different* 2026 task and therefore must be discarded solely on its name.
- **CANDIDATE ALIAS / IDENTITY AMBIGUOUS:** both names are used in 2026 official-site material for near-identical stated objectives, but direct canonical challenge IDs, resource lists, and full-statement revisions have not been reconciled.
- **NOT YET PROVEN:** full 14/14 semantic coverage of E193, accuracy of its subjective lunar rating, or planetary-analogue challenge superiority.

E411's arithmetic (planetary 4.59 rank 5/14, validation-axis sensitivity, 8.2 tie threshold) is unchanged. Its statement **'13/14 title coverage'** remains literally correct; its inference **'different lunar task'** is now downgraded to **unresolved alias/duplicate**.
